## The spectral physics of noise colors

Noise colors are defined mathematically by their power spectral density S(f), which describes how energy is distributed across the frequency spectrum as frequency (f) increases:

<Formula>
Power spectral density: S(f) ∝ 1 / (f^γ)
Power change per octave (dB) = −10 × γ × log10(2)
Power change per decade (dB) = −10 × γ
</Formula>

Where:
- **f** is the sound frequency in Hertz (Hz).
- **γ** (gamma) is the spectral exponent: 0 for white noise, 1 for pink noise, and 2 for brown (Brownian) noise.
- **Octave slope** indicates power drop each time frequency doubles (e.g. from 500 Hz to 1,000 Hz).
- **Decade slope** indicates power drop each time frequency multiplies tenfold (e.g. from 100 Hz to 1,000 Hz).

## Comparison of noise colors

| Noise color | Exponent (γ) | Slope per octave | Auditory character | Common use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White noise | 0 | 0 dB/oct | Crisp, hissy, like steady television static | Sound masking in loud offices; tinnitus relief |
| Pink noise | 1 | −3 dB/oct | Warm, balanced, like steady rainfall or rushing stream | Sleep soundscapes; study focus; audio equipment calibration |
| Brown noise | 2 | −6 dB/oct | Deep, heavy, like a distant waterfall or airplane cabin | Deep relaxation; meditation; soothing restless sleep |

## Safe listening practices

- **Keep volume below conversation level**: Aim for a volume equivalent to a quiet library or gentle shower (under 60 dBA).
- **Use sleep timers for overnight rest**: If you use sound to fall asleep, our built-in timer automatically ramps down the volume over the final 60 seconds so silence does not abruptly wake you.
- **Protect children and infants**: Pediatric ear canals amplify higher frequencies. Keep sound machines on the opposite side of the room from a baby’s crib at low settings.
