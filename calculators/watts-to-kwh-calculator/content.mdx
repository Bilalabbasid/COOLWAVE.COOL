Every household appliance draws electrical power, but electric utility bills are measured and charged in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Converting the wattage stamped on a device’s specification label into kilowatt-hours helps you anticipate electricity bills and discover which appliances consume the largest slice of your monthly budget.

This calculator converts watts (or kilowatts) and daily run times into total energy consumption per day, week, month, and year, with an optional duty cycle adjustment for cycling appliances like fridges and heat pumps.

## The watts to kWh conversion formula

According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), one kilowatt-hour represents 1,000 watts of electrical power delivered continuously for one hour:

<Formula>
Daily energy (kWh) = Power (watts) × Daily hours × (Duty cycle % ÷ 100) ÷ 1,000
Weekly energy (kWh) = Daily energy × Days used per week
Yearly energy (kWh) = (Weekly energy × 365) ÷ 7
Monthly energy (kWh) = Yearly energy ÷ 12
</Formula>

Where:
- **Power** is rated electrical draw in watts (W) or kilowatts (kW, where 1 kW = 1,000 W).
- **Daily hours** represents the total hours the appliance is energized per 24-hour day.
- **Duty cycle** is the percentage of on-time the compressor or heating element draws peak current.

## Worked example: 100-watt fixture used 10 hours daily

Consider a 100-watt fixture or entertainment center operating 10 hours every day with a continuous 100% duty cycle:

<WorkedExample slug="watts-to-kwh-calculator" inputs={{ powerUnit: 'W', watts: 100, hours: 10, duty: 100, daysPerWeek: 7 }} />

1. Power = 100 W; Daily run time = 10 hours.
2. Daily energy: (100 × 10 × 1.0) ÷ 1,000 = **1.000 kWh per day** (1,000 Wh).
3. Weekly energy: 1.000 × 7 = **7.00 kWh per week**.
4. Yearly energy: (7.00 × 365) ÷ 7 = **365 kWh per year**.
5. Average monthly energy: 365 ÷ 12 ≈ **30.4 kWh per month**.
6. This single load represents roughly 3.5% of an average U.S. home’s yearly electricity usage (about 10,500 kWh, EIA).
