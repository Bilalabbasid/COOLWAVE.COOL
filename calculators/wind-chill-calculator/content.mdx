Wind chill describes how cold outdoor air feels on exposed human skin when wind speeds accelerate heat loss. In calm winter air, your body forms a thin insulating layer of warmed air just above your skin. Wind sweeps this boundary layer away, speeding convective heat transfer and dramatically increasing the risk of frostbite and hypothermia.

This calculator implements the official 2001 Wind Chill Temperature Index adopted jointly by the U.S. National Weather Service (NWS) and Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC).

## The official 2001 wind chill formula

The JAG/TI wind chill index calculates apparent cold based on air temperature measured in the shade and wind velocity measured at the standard meteorological anemometer height of 10 meters (33 feet):

<Formula>
Imperial (°F, mph): Wind chill = 35.74 + 0.6215 × T − 35.75 × (V^0.16) + 0.4275 × T × (V^0.16)
Metric (°C, km/h): Wind chill = 13.12 + 0.6215 × T − 11.37 × (V^0.16) + 0.3965 × T × (V^0.16)
</Formula>

Where:
- **T** is ambient air temperature in °F (≤ 50 °F) or °C (≤ 10 °C).
- **V** is sustained wind speed in miles per hour (≥ 3 mph) or kilometers per hour (≥ 5 km/h).
- The exponent **0.16** accounts for the non-linear relationship between wind speed and facial skin cooling.

## Frostbite risk and time-to-freeze thresholds

Environment and Climate Change Canada provides standardized danger categories based on the wind chill equivalent temperature:

| Wind chill range (°F) | Wind chill range (°C) | ECCC risk category | Time for exposed skin to freeze |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 °F to 14 °F | 0 °C to −10 °C | Low | Frostbite not expected |
| 13 °F to −18 °F | −10 °C to −28 °C | Moderate | Possible with prolonged exposure |
| −19 °F to −40 °F | −28 °C to −40 °C | High | 10 to 30 minutes |
| −41 °F to −54 °F | −40 °C to −48 °C | Very high | 5 to 10 minutes |
| −55 °F to −67 °F | −48 °C to −55 °C | Severe | 2 to 5 minutes |
| Below −67 °F | Below −55 °C | Extreme | Less than 2 minutes |

## Winter exposure and hypothermia safety

- **Dress in dry layers**: Wear a moisture-wicking base layer against your skin, an insulating fleece or wool mid-layer, and a wind-blocking, waterproof outer shell. Wet clothing loses 90% of its insulating capability.
- **Cover exposed extremities**: The nose, ears, cheeks, fingers, and toes have the lowest blood volume during cold-induced vasoconstriction and are the first sites to freeze. Wear mittens rather than gloves to share finger warmth.
- **Factor in sunlight**: The official wind chill index assumes clear night conditions. Bright daytime sunshine can raise perceived warmth by 10 °F to 18 °F (6 °C to 10 °C).
